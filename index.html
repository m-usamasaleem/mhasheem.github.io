About

I am a final-year Computer Science student at FAST-NUCES Karachi (BS, 2022 to 2026), with A and A+ grades across my AI and quantitative coursework and three consecutive Dean's List terms. I am an Associate AI Researcher at Percevio Labs and a Section Leader for Stanford's Code in Place.

My thesis showed that a published multi-agent medical AI system, which succeeds on every task with frontier models, falls to 10 to 18% on the small local models hospitals can actually run privately. I rebuilt its orchestration so open 7B to 8B models reach 91%, fully offline on consumer CPUs. That work is now a collaboration with the system's original authors.

I am applying to PhD programs to study how to make AI systems trustworthy under real constraints: limited compute, private data, and humans who must stay in charge of the decisions that matter.

Enhancing mAIstro: reliable small models for multi-agent medical AI

Thesis · MICCAI 2027 target
Aug 2025 to present · FAST-NUCES, supervised by Ms. Alina Arshad · with the original mAIstro authors Dr. Michail E. Klontzas and Dr. Eleftherios Tzanis

Hospitals that cannot send patient data to external APIs need small local models, but mAIstro's perfect task success with frontier LLMs collapsed on them. I designed a Master Router Agent over 13+ sub-agents and an optimization pipeline of context pruning, enforced chain-of-thought, schema-constrained decoding, and agentic RAG over a 10,000+ line tool library.

GovMem: dependency-aware memory for AI course assistants

Ongoing
Sep 2026 to present · with Muhammad Usama Saleem (PhD, UNC Charlotte) and Dr. Ayman Ali (Riyadh College of Technology)

When an instructor corrects course content, today's AI tools do not carry the fix to the quizzes, grades and feedback that depend on it. GovMem traces which artifacts depend on each edit, updates only those, and routes uncertain changes to the instructor for approval. I am building CourseCorrect, a benchmark of semester-long course-edit episodes, to evaluate it.
